The [screener](/) lists every active mutual fund scheme in India with returns, SIP returns and risk calculated from official NAV history. Here is how to get the most from it.

## 1. Start with the default view

The table opens with **Direct plans** and the **Growth option** only, sorted by 3-year return. This is the cleanest group, because Regular plans cost more and IDCW options pay out part of the value, which distorts NAV-based returns. See [Direct vs Regular](/learn/direct-vs-regular-mutual-funds/) and [Growth vs IDCW](/learn/growth-vs-idcw-mutual-funds/). Use the **Plan** and **Option** filters to change this.

## 2. Pick a category

Funds are only fairly comparable within the same SEBI category. Open **Category** and choose one, for example "Flexi Cap". The default sort by 3-year return then ranks that group. See [mutual fund categories](/learn/mutual-fund-categories-india/).

## 3. Read several columns, not one

- **1Y, 3Y, 5Y**: returns. 1 year and above are annualised (CAGR). See [CAGR vs XIRR vs absolute return](/learn/cagr-xirr-absolute-return/).
- **SIP 3Y**: the return of a monthly SIP over the last three years.
- **Volatility** and **max drawdown**: how bumpy the ride was. See [risk measures](/learn/mutual-fund-risk-measures/).

Use **Columns** to add more, including 1-month to 10-year returns, Sharpe and Sortino ratios and [rolling returns](/learn/rolling-returns-explained/).

## 4. Narrow with ranges

Open **Ranges** to keep, for example, funds with 5-year return of at least 15% and volatility under 20%. Funds without a value for a metric are left out when you filter on it.

## 5. Open a fund

Click a fund name for its page: NAV chart, all returns with the fund's rank among its peers, SIP returns, risk and a similar-funds list.

## 6. Compare

Use [Compare](/compare/) to put two to four funds side by side with a chart that scales each to 100 at the start, so you can see which grew more over the same period.

## 7. Share or save

The page address holds your filters. Copy it to share. **Export CSV** downloads the current list.

## Good habits

- Compare like with like: same category, same plan, same option.
- Look at more than one period; a fund can top one list by luck.
- Check risk next to return.
- Look at the **TER** and **AUM** columns too. A lower expense ratio means less cost every year, and a very small AUM can mean a fund is new or not popular. Neither is a verdict on its own.
- Remember what the screener cannot show: exit load, portfolio and fund manager. Check these in the fund's own documents.
- Past performance does not indicate future returns. This site is educational and does not recommend any fund.

If you want to know how each number is calculated, read the [methodology](/methodology/).

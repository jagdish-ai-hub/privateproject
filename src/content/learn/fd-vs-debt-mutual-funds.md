A **fixed deposit (FD)** pays a rate fixed on the day you invest. A **[debt mutual fund](/learn/debt-mutual-funds-explained/)** holds bonds and money-market instruments, and its return depends on what those earn and how their prices move. Both are used for money that should not swing like the stock market, but they work differently.

## Side by side

| | Fixed deposit | Debt mutual fund |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return | Fixed when you invest | Not fixed; depends on interest and bond prices |
| Safety | Bank deposits insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (DICGC) | No insurance; risk depends on what the fund holds |
| Withdrawing early | Usually a penalty on the rate | Usually any day; some funds have a small exit load for early exit |
| Tax | Interest taxed at slab rate every year as it accrues | Gain taxed at slab rate only in the year you redeem (units bought from 1 April 2023) |
| Cost | No visible cost | Expense ratio, often 0.1% to 0.6% a year for Direct plans |

## A worked example

₹1,00,000 in an FD at 7% a year, compounded quarterly, grows to about **₹1,23,144** in 3 years. If you are in the 30% slab, about ₹6,943 of the ₹23,144 interest goes to tax, leaving **₹1,16,201**, an after-tax return of about 5.1% a year. You can check this in the [FD calculator](/tools/fd-calculator/).

A debt fund that earned the same 7% a year after costs would be worth about ₹1,22,504 after 3 years. Its gain is taxed at the same slab rate, but only when you sell, so the tax is paid once at the end instead of each year. The amounts after tax end up close; the debt fund's edge is the delay in paying tax and the freedom to withdraw any day. Its drawback is that 7% is not promised.

## What decides which fits

1. **Do you need a known amount on a known date?** For a school fee or a down payment due on a fixed date, an FD's certainty is worth a lot.
2. **Might you need the money suddenly?** Breaking an FD usually costs part of the interest. Most debt funds can be redeemed any working day.
3. **How much are you putting in one bank?** DICGC insurance covers ₹5 lakh per bank. Above that, spreading deposits or using high-quality debt funds spreads the risk.
4. **What is your tax slab?** At a 0% or 5% slab the tax difference hardly matters; at 30% the yearly taxation of FD interest matters more.

## Choosing a debt fund instead

Match the fund to your time frame. Liquid and overnight funds suit days to months. Short duration, corporate bond, and banking and PSU funds suit roughly one to three years. Longer-duration and credit risk funds can fall more when rates rise or a borrower struggles. Compare debt funds in the [screener](/) by 1-year and 3-year return, expense ratio and fund size.

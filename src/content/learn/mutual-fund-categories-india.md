SEBI's categorisation framework places every open-ended scheme into one defined category with its own investment rules. That is why this site's tables always show a category and why comparing within a category is the fair way to use them. The thresholds below are the main rules at the time of writing; SEBI can revise them, so check the current circular or AMFI for the exact wording.

## Equity categories

- **Large cap**: at least 80% in large-cap companies (the top 100 by market value).
- **Mid cap**: at least 65% in mid-cap companies (ranked 101 to 250).
- **Small cap**: at least 65% in small-cap companies (ranked 251 onward).
- **Large & mid cap**: at least 35% in each of large and mid caps.
- **Multi cap**: at least 25% in each of large, mid and small caps.
- **Flexi cap**: at least 65% in equity, with no restriction on company size.
- **Focused**: at most 30 stocks.
- **Value / contra**: follow a value or contrarian strategy.
- **Dividend yield**: mainly stocks that pay higher dividends.
- **Sectoral / thematic**: at least 80% in one sector or theme, such as banking or technology.
- **ELSS**: tax-saving funds with a three-year lock-in. See [ELSS](/learn/elss-tax-saving-mutual-funds/).

In general, small-cap funds are the most volatile and large-cap funds the least among these, and sectoral funds depend on one part of the economy.

## Hybrid categories

These mix equity and debt. They include **aggressive hybrid** (mostly equity), **conservative hybrid** (mostly debt), **balanced advantage / dynamic asset allocation** (the equity share changes with a model), **multi asset allocation** (equity, debt and gold or other assets), **equity savings** and **arbitrage** (which tries to profit from price gaps between cash and futures markets with low risk).

## Debt categories

Debt funds are grouped mainly by how long their bonds last and by credit quality: overnight, liquid, ultra short, low duration, money market, short, medium, long duration, corporate bond, credit risk, banking & PSU, gilt and others. See [debt mutual funds explained](/learn/debt-mutual-funds-explained/).

## Other categories

**Index funds and ETFs** track an index (see [index funds vs ETFs vs active funds](/learn/index-funds-vs-etfs-vs-active-funds/)), **fund of funds** invest in other funds (domestic or overseas), and **solution-oriented** schemes such as retirement and children's funds come with a lock-in.

## How AMFI's naming shows up here

AMFI's daily file uses the older names for some schemes (for example "Equity Schemes" and "Hybrid Scheme") and the newer names for others. This site merges the two into one name per category so that you see, for example, one "Small Cap" group.

## Using categories well

Filter by category in the [screener](/), then compare returns over several periods, risk and cost within it. A fund at the top of a high-risk category is not "better" than a fund in a lower-risk category; it just took more risk.

Almost every loan in India, whether for a home, a car or personal use, is repaid through **EMIs**: the same amount every month for a fixed number of months. The number looks simple, but how it is built decides how much the loan really costs.

## What is inside an EMI

Each month, the lender first charges interest on **the balance you still owe**, at one-twelfth of the yearly rate. Whatever is left of the EMI repays part of the loan, so the balance falls and next month's interest is a little smaller. Over time, more of each EMI goes to the loan and less to interest.

The formula is **EMI = P × i × (1 + i)ⁿ ÷ ((1 + i)ⁿ − 1)**, where P is the loan, i is the yearly rate divided by 12 and n is the number of months. You do not need to use it by hand: the [home loan EMI calculator](/tools/home-loan-emi-calculator/) does it and shows each year's split.

## Why early EMIs are mostly interest

At the start the balance is at its highest, so the interest is too. On a long home loan, the first years' payments can be mostly interest with only a small part repaying the loan. This matters if you plan to sell or switch lenders early: you may have repaid less of the loan than you expect. It also explains why prepaying early saves the most.

## Tenure: the cost of a lower EMI

Stretching a loan lowers the EMI but raises the total interest a lot, because the balance stays high for longer. For example, ₹50 lakh at 8.75% costs about ₹56 lakh in interest over 20 years, but about ₹40 lakh over 15 years. The EMI is higher on the shorter loan, so choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.

## Flat rate vs reducing-balance rate

Home loans are normally quoted on a reducing balance. Some car and personal loans are quoted as a **flat rate**. A flat rate charges interest on the full original amount for the whole term, even though you repay part of it every month. The EMI is the loan plus all that interest, divided by the number of months. The result is that a flat rate costs roughly **1.8 times** the same number on a reducing balance. An 8% flat rate on a 5-year loan is about 14% reducing.

When you compare offers, convert flat rates to the equivalent reducing-balance rate. The [car loan EMI calculator](/tools/car-loan-emi-calculator/) has a Flat rate option that does this.

## Fees and other costs

The **processing fee** is usually a percentage of the loan, paid once. It does not change the EMI, but it raises what the loan costs. Insurance, documentation and other charges can add more. Compare the total you will pay, not only the EMI. The [personal loan EMI calculator](/tools/personal-loan-emi-calculator/) adds the fee to the total.

## Prepayment

Paying extra reduces the balance, so less interest is charged from then on. With most loans, prepayment keeps the EMI the same and shortens the loan. The saving is bigger the earlier you prepay and the higher the interest rate. Rules on prepayment charges differ by loan type and lender, and they have changed over time, so check your agreement. Before prepaying, make sure you keep enough savings for emergencies, because prepaid money cannot be taken back out.

Prepaying is a risk-free way to "earn" the loan's interest rate, but whether it beats investing depends on your rate, your tax position and your comfort with risk.

## A sensible way to use the calculators

1. Enter the loan, the rate and the shortest tenure you can afford, and look at the **total interest**, not just the EMI.
2. Try a longer tenure to see what a lower EMI really costs.
3. For a flat-rate offer, switch to Flat rate and compare the equivalent rate with other lenders.
4. Try an extra monthly payment or a lump sum to see how much it saves.

A **SIP** (systematic investment plan) puts a fixed amount into a mutual fund every month. A **fixed deposit** locks a sum at a fixed rate. People compare them because both are common ways to save, but one is market-linked and the other is guaranteed. (If you save monthly in a deposit, the closer match is a recurring deposit: see [SIP vs RD](/learn/sip-vs-rd/).)

## The main differences

| | SIP in an equity fund | Fixed deposit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Return | Not fixed; follows the market | Fixed for the term |
| Can it fall? | Yes, sometimes for a year or more | No, the deposit does not fall |
| Inflation | Has historically beaten inflation over long periods | Often close to inflation after tax |
| Tax | On gains, only when you sell | Interest taxed every year at slab rate |
| Lock-in | None (except ELSS, 3 years) | Fixed term; early exit usually costs part of the interest |

## ₹5,000 a month for 5 years

Over 5 years you put in ₹3,00,000. In the [SIP calculator](/tools/sip-calculator/):

| Assumed return a year | Value after 5 years |
| --- | --- |
| 8% | about ₹3,69,834 |
| 10% | about ₹3,90,412 |
| 12% | about ₹4,12,432 |

A post office RD at 6.7% would turn the same ₹5,000 a month into about ₹3,56,829, guaranteed. Over 10 years, ₹5,000 a month at 12% would become about ₹11,61,695 on ₹6,00,000 invested. These assumed returns are for illustration: an equity SIP can return far more or far less, and it can be worth less than you put in at the end of a bad year.

## Which goal suits which

1. **Within 1 to 2 years** (a holiday, a gadget, an emergency fund top-up): an FD or RD, because a market fall right before you need the money would hurt.
2. **3 to 5 years**: a mix, or a hybrid or debt fund, depending on how much a fall would matter.
3. **More than 5 years** (retirement, a child's education): an equity SIP has the better long-run record, if you can stay invested through falls.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing a SIP's best year with an FD's rate. Look at [rolling returns](/learn/rolling-returns-explained/) to see how often an equity fund's returns were poor.
- Stopping a SIP after a fall, which locks in the lower prices.
- Ignoring tax: compare FD returns after tax using the [FD calculator](/tools/fd-calculator/).

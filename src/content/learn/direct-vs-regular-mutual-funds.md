Every mutual fund scheme in India is available in two **plans**: Regular and Direct. They invest in the same securities and are managed by the same team. The only difference is the cost.

## What changes

- **Regular plan**: bought through a distributor, bank or platform that is paid a commission. That commission is paid out of the scheme's expenses, so the [expense ratio](/learn/expense-ratio-and-exit-load/) is higher.
- **Direct plan**: bought straight from the fund house or from a platform that does not take a commission. There is no distribution cost, so the expense ratio is lower.

The extra cost shows up every day as a slightly lower NAV growth. Because the two plans have the same portfolio, the Direct plan's NAV is always higher than the Regular plan's for the same scheme.

## Why a small difference matters

The gap in expense ratio is often around half a percentage point to a full point for equity funds, and it compounds. Using the [SIP calculator](/tools/sip-calculator/) with an assumed 1 percentage point difference, a SIP of ₹10,000 a month for 20 years (₹24,00,000 invested) grows to about ₹99.9 lakh at 12% a year and about ₹87.4 lakh at 11%, a difference of about ₹12.6 lakh. For a one-time ₹1,00,000 over 20 years, it is about ₹9.6 lakh against ₹8.1 lakh.

These are illustrations based on assumed returns, not forecasts. The point is that a cost difference that looks small each year adds up over decades.

## How to tell which is which

The scheme name usually says "Direct Plan" or "Regular Plan". On this site the screener shows the plan under every fund name and filters to Direct plans by default so that like is compared with like. Mixing the two in one comparison would make the Regular plans look worse purely because of their cost.

## Which should you choose?

If you are comfortable choosing funds and investing online, a Direct plan saves the commission. If you value personal guidance and would otherwise not invest at all, a Regular plan through an advisor may still be the better outcome for you. Registered investment advisers charge a fee directly instead of taking a commission, which is a third route.

This is general education, not advice for your situation.

Comparing mutual funds well is mostly about comparing **like with like**, over **several periods**, and looking at **risk and cost** as well as returns. This guide goes through each step. You can do all of it on this site's [side-by-side page](/compare/) and [screener](/).

## 1. Compare funds that play the same game

Funds in different categories are built for different jobs, so their returns are not comparable. A small cap fund and a large cap fund, or an equity fund and a debt fund, will differ for reasons that have nothing to do with the fund manager. Keep three things the same:

- **Category**, such as large cap, flexi cap or corporate bond. See the [categories guide](/learn/mutual-fund-categories-india/).
- **Plan**: compare Direct with Direct. A Regular plan of the same fund always shows a lower return because its cost is higher. See [Direct vs Regular](/learn/direct-vs-regular-mutual-funds/).
- **Option**: compare Growth with Growth. IDCW payouts lower the NAV, so its returns look smaller.

## 2. Performance: look at more than one number

- **Returns over 1, 3 and 5 years.** Short periods swing with the market; longer ones say more about the fund.
- **Rolling returns.** Instead of one start date, these look at every possible 1-year or 3-year period and show the average and the worst. They reveal whether good returns were steady or came from one lucky stretch. See [rolling returns](/learn/rolling-returns-explained/).
- **SIP returns (XIRR).** If you invest monthly, this is the return a SIP actually earned.

Always compare over the same dates. The side-by-side page does this for you.

## 3. Against the benchmark

Every scheme names a benchmark index in its factsheet, and fund houses show the fund's return next to the benchmark's. Two quick checks:

- **Against the category.** Fund pages on this site show the fund's rank among similar funds and how it compares with the category median.
- **Against an index fund.** For an active large cap fund, compare it with a Nifty 50 or Nifty 100 index fund. If the active fund has not beaten the low-cost index fund over 3 to 5 years, its higher cost has not paid off. See [index funds vs active funds](/learn/index-funds-vs-etfs-vs-active-funds/).

## 4. Risk: how bumpy was the ride?

Two funds with the same return can feel very different to hold. Look at:

- **Worst fall from a peak** (maximum drawdown): the biggest drop you would have sat through.
- **Volatility**: how much returns swung around their average.
- **Sharpe ratio**: return earned for each unit of risk.

See [risk measures](/learn/mutual-fund-risk-measures/).

## 5. Cost and size

- **Expense ratio** is taken from the fund every year. Within a category, a lower cost is a head start that compounds.
- **Fund size (AUM)** matters at the extremes: a very small fund may be new or unpopular, and a very large small cap fund can find it harder to buy and sell.
- **Age**: a fund with less than 3 to 5 years of history has not been through a full market cycle.

## 6. Holdings, portfolio and overlap

What a fund owns explains how it behaves. Fund houses publish each scheme's full portfolio every month, and the factsheet lists the top holdings and sector weights. If you hold two funds, compare their top holdings: high **overlap** means you own much the same stocks twice and get little extra spread. This site does not show holdings, so read them in the factsheet.

## Comparing two schemes, step by step

1. Open the [side-by-side page](/compare/) and pick two to four funds from the same category, Direct plan, Growth option.
2. Read the growth chart: how the same amount would have grown, and where each fell.
3. Check the table: returns over several periods, SIP returns, worst fall, volatility, expense ratio and size.
4. Read "What the numbers say", which sums up who leads on what.
5. For holdings and the benchmark, open each fund's factsheet.

This is general information, not personal advice. Past performance does not promise future returns.
